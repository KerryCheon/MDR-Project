So getting the actual router to work is pretty much equivalent to making the single global model to work?

---

Yes, **exactly**. 

If the router is gating based on the **target value** (soil moisture), then predicting which bin the target falls into is mathematically equivalent to predicting the target itself. In other words, a perfect target-based router is just a quantized global regression model. If the global regression model is struggling due to noise and feature competition, the router will struggle just as much.

This reveals the core bottleneck of target-gated MoE: **you have made the routing task just as hard as the regression task.**

### How standard MoE actually works around this:
To make MoE useful, the routing must be based on **easily observable input features** (covariates) rather than the hidden target variable. 

For example, if you gate by:
1. **Physical context** (e.g. routing by season, soil texture class, or geographic region): The router's job becomes trivial (100% accuracy based on simple inputs like DOY or station ID).
2. **Meteorological conditions** (e.g. routing based on whether it has rained in the last 48 hours): This decouples the regimes without requiring the router to estimate the exact soil moisture.

In these setups, the router succeeds easily because it isn't trying to solve the regression problem under the hood. The specialists then get to focus on local physical dynamics (e.g., dry-down evaporation rates vs. wet infiltration rates) without competing for feature splits.

### 1. Why Predictions Have a Singular Peak (Generalization Collapse)
We trained and evaluated the expert models on both the training (`trainval`) and testing splits to check if the models behave differently. 

* **On the training set**, the models do **not** output a singular peak. The predicted standard deviation almost perfectly matches the true target standard deviation (e.g., for $k=3$ Bin 1: target std = `0.0421`, pred std = `0.0406`), and the Train MAE is extremely low (`0.0027`).
* **On the test set**, however, the prediction variance collapses by **35% to 45%** (e.g., Test Pred Std drops to `0.0255` against a Test Target Std of `0.0410`), and the Test MAE is **12x higher** (`0.0338`).

**Diagnosis**: The models are **massively overfitting**. Because each expert model is trained on a small subset of samples (e.g. only ~5,800 to ~7,700 training samples) but using a very large feature set (**499 features**), the decision trees overfit to specific feature combinations present only in the training years. When presented with unseen test data, these specific feature combinations do not generalize, so the trees default to more general paths, reverting all predictions back to the central training mean. This creates the narrow **singular peak** in the test predictions.

### 2. Why Expert 1 Fails to Capture the 0.025 Peak and Peaks at 0.075
This is a direct result of the generalization collapse combined with a **target distribution shift** between training and testing splits in the dry regime (Bin 1):

* **The Target Shift**: 
  * In the training set (`trainval`), the dry regime is relatively wet: only **33.15%** of samples are $\le 0.05$ (median target of **0.0770**).
  * In the test set, the dry regime is significantly drier: **50.52%** of samples are $\le 0.05$, creating a heavy true peak around **0.025** (median target of **0.0490**).
* **The Generalization Failure**:
  * On training data, Expert 1 successfully identifies dry samples and predicts a mean of **0.0272** for true targets $\le 0.05$.
  * On test data, because of the overfitting described above, the model fails to track the features representing dry soil moisture and reverts to the central tendency of its training experiences (around the training median of **0.0770**).
  * As a result, the model outputs a singular peak around **0.075 - 0.082** for the test set, completely missing the true test target peak at **0.025**.

This reveals that the specialists are highly fragile to target/covariate shifts, which explains why a slight routing error leads to large prediction spikes in E2E gating.
